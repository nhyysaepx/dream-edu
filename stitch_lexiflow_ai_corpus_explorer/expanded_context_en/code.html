Investigating the Role of Morphosyntactic Complexity in Second Language Acquisition

The present study aims to delineate the intricate relationship between exposure to rich input and the subsequent production of complex syntactic structures in adult L2 learners. While previous research has firmly established that vocabulary acquisition is highly sensitive to frequency effects, the acquisition of structural rules—particularly those governing morphosyntactic phenomena—appears to follow a more nuanced trajectory. Our longitudinal analysis reveals that learners frequently produce morphosyntactic errors not due to a lack of conceptual understanding, but rather due to processing constraints when mapping functional morphology onto lexical roots in real-time discourse. Furthermore, the data suggests that targeted pedagogical interventions focusing on form-meaning mappings significantly accelerate the restructuring of the learner's interlanguage.

Morphosyntactic errors

Mistakes related to how words are formed (morphology) and how they are arranged into sentences (syntax). For example, saying "He walk to store" instead of "He walks to the store" involves both incorrect word endings and missing structural words.

lightbulb Why it matters here

The author argues these errors aren't just memory failures, but happen because the brain is overloaded trying to combine word meanings and grammar rules simultaneously while speaking.
